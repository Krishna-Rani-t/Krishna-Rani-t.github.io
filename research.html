Master’s thesis

Toward Robust LLM Benchmarking in High School Science Education: Constructing a QA Corpus from Standardized Tests

Leibniz Universität Hannover, March–September 2026. Advisors: Dr. Jennifer D’Souza and Prof. Dr. Sören Auer.

General-purpose benchmarks say little about how reliable a model is at a specific school level. The thesis introduces HighSchoolBench, a multimodal benchmark of 10,501 authentic questions from 25 assessments and competitions covering Grades 9–12 across 11 subjects. 28.1% of the questions are open-ended and 27.6% need an image to answer.

What I built

  • A reusable PDF-to-JSON pipeline that combines OCR (Chandra, DeepSeek-OCR), source-specific preprocessing, extraction with Qwen3-32B, automated checks and human validation.
  • The benchmark itself: structured JSON records with linked images and tables, ready for automated evaluation.
  • An evaluation tool that ran 22 instruction-tuned and reasoning models, including Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek and GPT-OSS, on 8,393 test questions in zero-shot and few-shot settings, through OpenAI-compatible APIs on SLURM-scheduled H100 and L40S GPUs.

What I found

  • Reasoning models performed best on multiple-choice questions.
  • Open-ended questions, image-dependent questions and worked solutions remained challenging.
  • Few-shot prompting gave inconsistent improvements.
  • Strong multiple-choice scores alone are not enough to judge whether a model is reliable for high-school education.

Research software

Since December 2024 I’ve worked as a student assistant in the SciKnowOrg group at TIB, which builds AI systems that turn scientific literature into machine-actionable knowledge. My part is keeping two of the group’s open-source Python libraries in good shape.

OntoLearner

A modular library for ontology learning with LLMs. I integrated the top four solutions from the LLMs4OL Challenge at ISWC as 11 reusable learner models across term typing, taxonomy discovery and Text2Onto. The original code was written for specific challenge datasets; I generalised it so each learner works with any ontology in the library.
